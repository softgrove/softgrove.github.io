Cabinet Cut List Template: Parts, Fields and Sheet Handoff

A cabinet cut list is the manufacturing source of truth between the drawing and the sheet layout. It should identify each finished part clearly enough that the diagram, shop copy and physical panel all refer to the same thing.

Use one row per distinct cabinet part and columns for cabinet ID, part name, finished width, finished length, quantity, material, thickness, grain direction, finished face and edge treatment. Group rows by interchangeable stock, then transfer part ID, dimensions, quantity and orientation constraints into a cutting layout. Use fields that survive the trip to the shop

Assign a stable cabinet and part ID rather than relying only on names such as side or shelf. Record finished width and length in one declared unit, plus quantity, material and thickness. Add separate fields for grain direction, finished face and edge treatment when those details affect how a rectangle may be placed.

Keep rough-cut or final-trim allowances out of the finished dimension fields. Put them in dedicated notes so the layout does not silently treat an oversize blank as a completed part.

  • Cabinet ID and unique part ID
  • Plain-English part name
  • Finished width, length and quantity
  • Material, thickness and finish
  • Grain orientation or allowed rotation
  • Finished face, edge treatment and revision notes

Separate boxes, fronts and non-interchangeable stock

Cabinet sides, shelves, backs and doors may use different materials, thicknesses or visible faces. Group only parts that can come from the same stock. A rectangle that fits geometrically is not a valid substitute when the material or finish is wrong.

Doors and matched fronts often carry appearance constraints that hidden cabinet parts do not. Record those constraints per part instead of locking every row in the list.

Audit the cabinet list before buying sheets

Count every required part and compare the quantities with the drawing. Confirm that width and length use the same convention throughout, and verify material, thickness, face and grain notes. Then check that each generated rectangle carries the same part ID as its source row.

When a design dimension changes, update the source list and regenerate the layout. Patching only the cutting diagram creates two competing versions of the cabinet dimensions.

Questions

What columns should a cabinet cut list include?

Include cabinet ID, part ID, part name, finished width and length, quantity, material, thickness, grain or rotation, finished face, edge treatment and revision notes.

Should cabinet cut list dimensions include kerf?

No. Keep finished dimensions in the cut list and apply measured kerf between parts in the sheet layout.

Can I download a printable cabinet cut list?

Use the linked woodworking cut list PDF as the printable base. Add cabinet IDs and face or edge notes in the available name and notes fields.

How do I turn the cabinet list into a plywood layout?
