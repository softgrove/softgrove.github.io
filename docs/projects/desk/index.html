Desk cut list

A 60" wide, 30" deep, 29" tall desk built from panel legs and a single 3/4" plywood top.

Project details

Panel-leg desks are the easiest desk to build from a single sheet: a top, two side panels, and a modesty panel at the back to keep it square. The side panels are 28.25" tall so the finished height with the top is 29", the standard for a writing desk.

The side panels are 23.5" deep rather than the full 30" so the top overhangs front and back, and so that both panels stack beside the top with a saw kerf between them and still clear the 48" sheet width. The modesty panel runs between the sides and sits back 1" from the rear edge.

Cutting sequence

  1. Cut the top

    A 60 × 30 top comes off the sheet as one rip and one crosscut. Cut it first so the best face lands on top.

  2. Cut the side panels from the rest

    The strip beside the top is just under 36 × 48. Both 28.25 × 23.5 sides stack in it with 1/8" to spare after the kerf.

  3. Cut the long strips

    The modesty panel and cleat are 58.5" long. They fit in the 18" strip next to the top.

  4. Band before assembly

    Edge-band the top and side panels before joining so you can trim flush on a flat surface.

Questions

Does everything fit on one sheet?

Yes, at a 1/8" kerf. The calculator shows the layout and reports about 87 percent yield.

Can I make the desk 72" wide?

The sides still fit, but a 72 × 30 top plus the panels pushes you to two sheets. Use the extra width of the second sheet for a drawer box or shelf.
