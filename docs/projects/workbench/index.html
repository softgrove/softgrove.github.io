Workbench cut list

A 60" long, 24" deep, 34" tall shop bench. Two layers of 3/4" plywood for the top, a plywood lower shelf, and a 2×4 frame, all from one sheet and five studs.

Project details

The plywood parts are two identical top layers and one lower shelf. Doubling the top gives a 1.5" slab that takes dog holes and a vise without flexing. The layers are 23.75" rather than a full 24" deep: two 24" rips plus a saw kerf come to 48.125", which is 1/8" more than the sheet. Shaving a quarter inch keeps the whole bench on one panel.

The frame is dimensional 2×4. Those cuts only need a length, so they go through the board calculator. The lumber list is further down the page.

Cutting sequence

  1. Rip the top layers

    Two rips at 23.75" use the full 48" width of the sheet with the kerf accounted for. Crosscut both to 60".

  2. Cut the shelf from the offcut

    The remaining piece is about 36 × 48. The 46.5 × 18 shelf comes out of it with the grain running across the bench.

  3. Cut the frame lumber

    Run the 2×4 list through the board calculator: five 8 ft studs, with a leg and a long rail sharing each of the first four.

  4. Assemble upside down

    Screw the legs to the underside of the doubled top, then flip the bench and add the lower rails and shelf.

Questions

Can I use MDF for the top?

Yes for the top layer if the bench stays dry. Keep the same dimensions. Some markets sell MDF at 49 × 97, so check the sheet size field before you trust the layout.

How many 2×4s does the frame take?

Five 8 ft studs at a 1/8" kerf. Each of the first four carries one 32.5" leg and one 54" rail; the fifth holds all four 18" short rails.

Why not a 72" bench?

A 72 × 30 top does not double up on one sheet: the second layer needs a second panel and the shelf a third. If you want the bigger bench, set the quantity to 2 on a 72 × 30 part and the calculator will show the three-sheet layout.
